\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a method for adapting foundation models to multi-modal maritime semantic segmentation under varying sensor conditions.
By building on MemorySAM's memory-based cross-modal framework, we identified and addressed two key failure modes: (1) soft MoE gating converges to uniform expert routing, producing modality-agnostic features---resolved by an MI routing loss that drives modality-specific expert specialization; and (2) the segmentation objective alone cannot train adaptive fusion weights---resolved by quality-aware cross-modal scoring with gating oracle supervision via per-modality auxiliary heads.
Combined with aggressive RGB corruption augmentations for day-to-night generalization, our experiments on the MULTIAQUA benchmark validate each design choice and demonstrate the importance of explicit training supervision for both expert routing and modality gating in degraded sensing conditions.
