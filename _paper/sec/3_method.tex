Our method adapts MemorySAM~\cite{chen2025memorysam} for the MULTIAQUA maritime segmentation task.
We retain the core idea of treating each modality as a sequential frame processed through SAM2's~\cite{ravi2024sam2} memory-based tracking pipeline, and modify the LoRA adaptation strategy, modality scoring, and output fusion.
Our pipeline operates in four phases: given $M$ input modalities (RGB, thermal, LiDAR), (1) all modalities are encoded by SAM2's frozen image encoder with Soft-MoE LoRA layers, where an MI routing loss drives modality-specific expert specialization; (2) a cross-modal fusion head jointly compares the backbone features and produces softmax-normalized relative weights; (3) these weights are re-normalized with a higher temperature and scaled by $M$ to modulate vision features before SAM2's memory-augmented tracking step (UAMM); and (4) the per-modality mask outputs are fused using the raw softmax weights (AMF).


\subsection{Soft-MoE LoRA with MI Specialization}
\label{sec:soft_moe_lora}

We keep SAM2's image encoder frozen and inject low-rank adaptation matrices into the query and value projections of each attention block, following the standard LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} formulation.
Rather than using a single pair of low-rank matrices, we maintain $N$ expert pairs $\{(\mathbf{A}_i, \mathbf{B}_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$, where $\mathbf{A}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r}$ and $\mathbf{B}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d}$, with $r$ being the LoRA rank and $d$ the feature dimension.

Following the Soft Mixture-of-Experts formulation~\cite{puigcerver2024softmoe}, instead of selecting the top-$k$ experts via hard routing~\cite{zhu2024moe, shazeer2017moe}, we compute a softmax-weighted combination over all experts for each input token $\mathbf{x}$:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \mathbf{x} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \alpha_i(\mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{B}_i \mathbf{A}_i \mathbf{x},
  \quad \alpha_i(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{\exp(g_i(\mathbf{x}))}{\sum_{j=1}^{N} \exp(g_j(\mathbf{x}))},
  \label{eq:soft_moe}
\end{equation}
where $g(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}_g \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_g$ is a lightweight gating network with $\mathbf{W}_g \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$.
The output of the original QKV projection is augmented as $\mathbf{q} \leftarrow \mathbf{q} + \Delta \mathbf{q}$ and $\mathbf{v} \leftarrow \mathbf{v} + \Delta \mathbf{v}$.
When the number of experts $N$ is small (\eg, $N{=}3$), hard top-$k$ routing with $k{=}2$ permanently disables one expert per token; soft gating avoids this by ensuring gradient flow to every expert at every step.
We use $N{=}3$ experts and $r{=}4$ throughout our experiments.

\noindent\textbf{MI routing loss.}
However, soft gating introduces a different problem: without explicit specialization pressure, all experts receive similar gradients and the gate converges to a near-uniform distribution (\eg, $\boldsymbol{\alpha} \approx [0.33, 0.33, 0.34]$ for all modalities).
When every modality passes through the same weighted combination of experts, the resulting backbone features become modality-agnostic, making it impossible for the downstream fusion head to distinguish modality quality.

To address this, we maximize the mutual information (MI) between the modality identity and the expert selection.
During encoding, we collect each modality's gate distribution $\boldsymbol{\alpha}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$ by averaging the softmax gate weights across all spatial positions and all Soft-MoE LoRA layers.
The MI is decomposed as $I = H(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}) - H(\boldsymbol{\alpha} \mid \text{modality})$, where:
\begin{equation}
  H(\boldsymbol{\alpha} \mid \text{mod.}) = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M} \left[-\sum_{n=1}^{N} \alpha_{i,n} \log \alpha_{i,n}\right],
  \label{eq:cond_ent}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  H(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}) = -\sum_{n=1}^{N} \bar{\alpha}_n \log \bar{\alpha}_n,
  \;\; \bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M} \boldsymbol{\alpha}_i.
  \label{eq:marg_ent}
\end{equation}
The MI routing loss minimizes the negative MI:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{MI} = H(\boldsymbol{\alpha} \mid \text{mod.}) - H(\bar{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}).
  \label{eq:mi_loss}
\end{equation}
Minimizing $\mathcal{L}_\text{MI}$ simultaneously reduces the conditional entropy (each modality concentrates on fewer experts) and increases the marginal entropy (all experts are utilized overall), driving modality-specific expert specialization while preventing expert collapse.


\subsection{Adaptive Cross-Modal Fusion}
\label{sec:cross_modal}

\noindent\textbf{Quality-aware scoring.}
A central challenge in multi-modal fusion is estimating the relative quality of each modality.
Prior approaches~\cite{chen2025memorysam} use independent per-modality heads (\eg, a CNN followed by sigmoid activation), but when backbone features are rich, the sigmoid saturates---all scores converge to ${\sim}1.0$ regardless of actual quality differences.
Moreover, global average pooling alone encodes semantic content rather than quality: a degraded RGB frame and a clear one may yield similar GAP vectors after backbone processing.

We address both issues by enriching each modality's representation with quality-sensitive statistics.
Given backbone feature maps $\{\mathbf{F}_i\}_{i=1}^{M}$, we compute a multi-pool descriptor for each modality:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{q}_i = [\operatorname{GAP}(\mathbf{F}_i)\,;\,\operatorname{GMP}(\mathbf{F}_i)\,;\,\sigma(\mathbf{F}_i)],
  \label{eq:multipool}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{GAP}$ and $\operatorname{GMP}$ denote global average and max pooling respectively, and $\sigma(\mathbf{F}_i) \in \mathbb{R}^{C}$ is the channel-wise standard deviation.
Each descriptor $\mathbf{q}_i \in \mathbb{R}^{3C}$ is compressed by a modality-specific network $\phi_i$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_i = \phi_i(\mathbf{q}_i) = \operatorname{ReLU}(\mathbf{W}_c^{(i)} \cdot \mathbf{q}_i + \mathbf{b}_c^{(i)}),
  \label{eq:compress}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}_c^{(i)} \in \mathbb{R}^{h \times 3C}$ are modality-specific weights.
The compressed vectors are concatenated and passed through a comparison MLP $\psi$ to produce modality weights:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{l} = \psi([\mathbf{z}_1; \ldots; \mathbf{z}_M]),
  \quad w_i = \frac{\exp(l_i / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^{M} \exp(l_j / \tau)},
  \label{eq:cross_modal}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is a temperature parameter.
The output layer of $\psi$ is zero-initialized so that all modalities receive uniform weight $1/M$ at the start of training.

\noindent\textbf{Gating oracle supervision.}
\label{sec:oracle}
The segmentation objective alone provides an insufficient gradient signal to drive adaptive gating: a network assigning near-constant weights already minimizes the main loss because the downstream mask fusion can compensate.
To provide a direct per-image training signal, we attach a lightweight auxiliary segmentation head $\mathcal{H}_i$ to each modality's backbone features.
We derive oracle gating weights from their predictions:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{w}_i = \frac{\exp(-\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}^{(i)})}{\sum_{j=1}^{M} \exp(-\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}^{(j)})},
  \label{eq:oracle}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{aux}^{(i)}$ is the per-image cross-entropy loss for modality $i$'s auxiliary head.
The fusion head is trained to match these oracle weights via KL divergence:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{gate} = D_\text{KL}(\tilde{\mathbf{w}} \,\|\, \mathbf{w}),
  \label{eq:gate_kl}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}$ is detached from the computation graph.
Combined with the MI routing loss that differentiates backbone features across modalities (\cref{sec:soft_moe_lora}), the oracle supervision ensures that the fusion head learns to translate these feature differences into input-adaptive gating weights.

\noindent\textbf{UAMM and AMF.}
The cross-modal weights are applied in two complementary paths.
For \textit{Uncertainty-Aware Memory Modulation} (UAMM), the raw logits $\mathbf{l}$ are re-normalized with a higher temperature $\tau_u$ and scaled by $M$:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{w}_i = M \cdot \frac{\exp(l_i / \tau_u)}{\sum_{j=1}^{M} \exp(l_j / \tau_u)},
  \quad \hat{\mathbf{f}}_i^{(k)} = \hat{w}_i \cdot \mathbf{f}_i^{(k)},
  \label{eq:uamm}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_u{=}2.0$.
The $M$-scaling preserves the average feature magnitude at $1.0$: when all modalities are equally reliable, $\hat{w}_i = 1.0$ for all; when one degrades, its features are suppressed while the best modality is amplified.
The modulated features are processed by SAM2's \texttt{track\_step}, where the first modality initializes the memory and subsequent modalities attend to it via cross-attention.

For \textit{Adaptive Mask Fusion} (AMF), the per-modality mask predictions $\mathbf{o}_i$ from the tracking loop are fused using the raw softmax weights:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{o}_\text{fused} = \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_i \cdot \mathbf{o}_i.
  \label{eq:amf}
\end{equation}
UAMM uses a softer distribution ($\tau_u > \tau$) to gently redistribute feature magnitudes, while AMF uses the sharper distribution from $\tau$ for decisive output weighting.


\subsection{Training Objectives}
\label{sec:training}

Following MemorySAM~\cite{chen2025memorysam}, we use a Semantic Prototype Memory Module: for each class $c$, a global prototype $\mathbf{p}_c$ is maintained via exponential moving average (momentum $= 0.8$), and the prototype loss is $\mathcal{L}_\text{proto} = \text{MSE}(\hat{\mathbf{p}}, \mathbf{p})$.
The total training loss is:
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
  \mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_\text{OHEM} + \mathcal{L}_\text{proto}
  + \lambda_\text{gate}\!\left(\mathcal{L}_\text{gate} + \gamma \cdot \textstyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i} \mathcal{L}_\text{aux}^{(i)}\right)
  + \lambda_\text{MI} \cdot \mathcal{L}_\text{MI},
  \end{split}
  \label{eq:total_loss}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{OHEM}$ is the Online Hard Example Mining cross-entropy loss, $\gamma{=}0.3$, $\lambda_\text{gate}{=}0.5$, and $\lambda_\text{MI}{=}1.0$.


\subsection{Night-Robust RGB Augmentation}
\label{sec:night_aug}

The MULTIAQUA dataset requires generalization from daytime training to nighttime evaluation, where RGB quality degrades severely while thermal and LiDAR remain relatively stable.
Inspired by~\cite{muhovic2025multiaqua} and CRM~\cite{shin2024crm}, we apply three complementary RGB corruptions during training (leaving thermal and LiDAR unchanged):

\noindent\textbf{Night Simulation} (probability 0.35): extreme brightness reduction (factor sampled from $[0.03, 0.25]$), followed by contrast reduction ($[0.3, 0.7]$), gamma correction ($\gamma \in [0.4, 0.8]$), and additive Gaussian noise ($\sigma = 0.02$).

\noindent\textbf{Complementary Random Masking} (probability 0.30): rectangular patches are randomly placed on the RGB image, zeroing out 20--50\% of spatial regions, forcing the model to rely on thermal and LiDAR for the masked areas.

\noindent\textbf{Full-Channel Zeroing} (probability 0.12): the entire RGB input is replaced with zeros, simulating complete RGB failure.

These augmentations are stacked sequentially: an image may undergo night simulation and then additionally be partially masked, creating diverse degradation patterns.
