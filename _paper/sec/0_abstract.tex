\begin{abstract}
Adapting vision foundation models to multi-modal semantic segmentation is challenging because these models are designed for single-modal input, and naively fusing heterogeneous modalities often fails when sensor quality varies across conditions.
We propose a method that adapts SAM2 via MemorySAM's memory-based cross-modal aggregation and addresses a key failure mode of multi-modal fusion with foundation models: when per-modality confidence is estimated independently via sigmoid activation, all scores saturate to ${\sim}1.0$ for rich backbone features, effectively disabling modality-adaptive weighting.
We replace independent scoring with a cross-modal fusion head that jointly compares all modality features through a shared compression and comparison network, producing softmax-normalized relative weights that inherently differentiate between modalities.
These weights are applied through two complementary paths: max-normalized for uncertainty-aware memory modulation---preserving the best modality's features at full strength while suppressing degraded ones---and as raw softmax for adaptive output fusion.
Combined with Soft Mixture-of-Experts gating in SAM2's LoRA layers for full expert utilization, and aggressive RGB corruption augmentations for day-to-night generalization, we validate each design choice on the MULTIAQUA benchmark~\cite{muhovic2025multiaqua}, achieving 94.00\% mIoU on the daytime validation set.

\end{abstract}
