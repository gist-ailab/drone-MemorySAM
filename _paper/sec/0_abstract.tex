\begin{abstract}
Adapting vision foundation models to multi-modal semantic segmentation is challenging because these models are designed for single-modal input, and naively fusing heterogeneous modalities often fails when sensor quality varies across conditions.
We propose a method that adapts SAM2 via MemorySAM's memory-based cross-modal aggregation and addresses two key failure modes of multi-modal fusion with foundation models.
First, when Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LoRA layers are used for modality-adaptive encoding, soft gating causes all experts to converge to a uniform routing distribution, producing modality-agnostic features that prevent downstream fusion from distinguishing sensor quality.
We resolve this by maximizing the mutual information (MI) between modality identity and expert routing, driving each modality to utilize distinct experts and producing differentiated backbone features.
Second, the segmentation objective alone provides insufficient gradient signal for the fusion head to learn input-adaptive gating weights.
We introduce quality-aware cross-modal scoring---enriching each modality's representation with multi-pool statistics through modality-specific compression networks---combined with gating oracle supervision via KL divergence from per-modality auxiliary segmentation heads.
The resulting weights are applied with temperature-scaled normalization for memory modulation and as raw softmax for output fusion.
To bridge the day-night domain gap, we apply aggressive RGB corruption augmentations including extreme darkening, complementary random masking~\cite{shin2024crm}, and stochastic full-channel zeroing.
We validate each design choice on the MULTIAQUA benchmark~\cite{muhovic2025multiaqua}, achieving 94.00\% mIoU on the daytime validation set.

\end{abstract}
