\subsection{Where Sensor Over-Reliance Comes From}
A fusion model can lean on one sensor because its architecture or supervision favours that sensor, or because the training data make that sensor sufficient. DGFusion~\cite{dgfusion2026} is a useful test case: it adds depth supervision to its fusion, and removing depth costs our reproduction of DGFusion 23 to 37 points depending on the condition (Fig.~\ref{fig:dependence}). If the depth supervision caused this dependence, a model without it should behave differently.

Our model with clean training only, which has no depth supervision, shows the same pattern: removing depth costs 23.5 to 38.7 points, and the cost grows from sun to low exposure as for DGFusion. The same model trained with the C2D2 scheme loses only 3.4 to 4.4 points in sun, cloud, rain, and fog, 10.1 at night, and 13.4 at low exposure. The effect also appears in a second architecture: when we re-train DGFusion with the same synthetic degradation, removing depth entirely costs 4.7 points, whereas it costs 33.3 points for our reproduction trained without degradation and 33.4 for the released weights (test split, our evaluation code; \todo{re-measure with the benchmark code of Table~\ref{tab:robust}}).

These measurements suggest that, on DELIVER, the dependence on depth comes mainly from training without degraded sensors rather than from a particular fusion design or supervision \todo{final wording after FU-04 (C2D2 applied to DGFusion and CAFuser) and FU-05 (all curves on one split)}. A plausible reason is that DELIVER renders depth without noise or holes, so training on complete inputs finds a shortcut that real depth sensors would not offer. The comparison has two caveats: the clean-trained model was trained for 200 epochs and the C2D2-trained model (without the high-resolution path) for 40, and the DGFusion curve was measured on the test split while ours were measured on validation.

\subsection{What the High-Resolution Path Adds}
The high-resolution path raises clean test mIoU by 1.15 points on average over three seeds (from 56.59 to 57.74 on the test split). Fig.~\ref{fig:thin} shows where the gain comes from. Averaged over the three seeds, Static gains 10.4 points, Pedestrian 3.8, TrafficSign 3.1, and Pole 2.1, and the four thin classes gain 3.0 points together. The gain comes mainly from recall: for Pole the recall rises from 0.75 to 0.78 and for Pedestrian from 0.89 to 0.90 \todo{confirm these recall values (two seeds, NAS analysis\_logs/a2\_thin\_anatomy\_20261018/summary.md) through the analysis session before citing}. Two classes lose: TrafficLight by 4.1 points and RailTrack by 7.2. RailTrack varies strongly between seeds even without the path (58.5, 32.6, and 45.1 for the three seeds), so we do not interpret its change. The TrafficLight loss, in contrast, is systematic and is analysed next.

\subsection{Failure Case: Posters Read as Traffic Lights}
With the high-resolution path, the recall of TrafficLight rises (0.80 to 0.85) but its precision falls (0.46 to 0.41) \todo{same confirmation as above}. Fig.~\ref{fig:failure} shows where the extra false positives appear. In the scenes with the largest increase, billboards and posters, labelled as \emph{Other}, are painted as traffic lights, and in one rainy scene a traffic sign is. The two classes share fine structure, a small bright rectangular shape on a pole or a wall, but differ in context. Because the high-resolution features are added to every pyramid level without a gate, fine detail reaches the dense per-pixel classifier directly, and the classifier can follow the detail without enough context. This mechanism is a hypothesis that our measurements are consistent with but do not prove; we discuss a remedy in Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}.

\subsection{Limitations}
First, DELIVER is synthetic, and its complete depth is the reason the shortcut exists; the size of the effect on real depth sensors remains to be measured, and MUSES does not contain depth. Second, our main runs use a 40-epoch schedule and three seeds, the ablations one seed, and the baselines are single runs, so differences below about one point should not be over-read. Third, whether the high-resolution path, whose convolutional stem sees raw pixels, changes robustness to pixel noise has to be checked under the benchmark noise levels \todo{NM results of Table~\ref{tab:robust} under the benchmark code pending}. Fourth, the frozen ViT-L processes every sensor separately, so inference costs 14.6 times the operations and 2.9 times the latency of DGFusion (Table~\ref{tab:cost}); sharing tokens across sensors or a smaller backbone would reduce this, which we have not evaluated.
