\PARstart{A}{utonomous} vehicles and mobile robots carry several sensors because each one fails in different conditions. A camera loses contrast at night and in fog, a depth estimate develops holes on reflective surfaces, an event camera goes silent on static scenes, and LiDAR returns thin out in rain and with distance. Multimodal semantic segmentation fuses these sensors so that the scene is still understood when one of them degrades.

The fusion models that lead the full-sensor benchmarks~\cite{cmnext2023,cmx2023,stitchfusion2024,geminifusion2024,cafuser2025,dgfusion2026} handle adverse conditions by telling the fusion what the condition is. CAFuser~\cite{cafuser2025} infers a condition token from the camera image and supervises it with a textual description of the weather and lighting, and DGFusion~\cite{dgfusion2026} adds local depth tokens and depth supervision. These models see the adverse scenes contained in their training data, but they are not trained with degraded or missing sensors, and they lose much of their accuracy when a sensor actually fails: on DELIVER, the released DGFusion weights fall to 7.5 mean intersection over union (mIoU) when only the RGB camera is left (Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}b). Methods designed for missing sensors~\cite{magic2024,anyseg2024,rmmss2025,fuseadapt2026} train with modality dropout and distillation, but the two lines of work rarely evaluate the same models under the same protocol~\cite{mmssbench2025}. Frozen vision foundation models offer strong features for every sensor, yet their multimodal adaptations mostly keep RGB as the primary stream and inject one auxiliary sensor~\cite{mmsamadapter2025}.

We ask whether a change of the training signal, instead of additional condition supervision, can give an accurate fusion model the ability to hold up when its sensors degrade. We propose Clean-to-Degraded Two-Pass Distillation (C2D2). Every training batch is used twice, once with clean inputs and once with sensor-specific synthetic degradation, such as blur and exposure changes for the camera, holes for depth, dropped beams for LiDAR, and lost resolution for events, together with entire sensor removal. The degraded pass is supervised by the labels and distilled from a frozen teacher that sees the clean input. C2D2 needs no condition or degradation labels and adds no computation at inference. We apply it to a fusion model that reads every sensor with one frozen DINOv3 backbone~\cite{dinov3_2025} through sensor-specific low-rank adapters, fuses the sensors with cross-modal attention, and recovers thin structures with a high-resolution decoding path.

On DELIVER~\cite{cmnext2023}, the model reaches 57.74 mIoU on the test set (mean of three training runs), 1.0 point above DGFusion (56.7), the best published four-sensor fusion model, and under the public missing-sensor benchmark~\cite{mmssbench2025} its mIoU averaged over all subsets of removed sensors is 58.52 on the validation set, 13.6 points above the best method reported there. On the real MUSES benchmark, it reaches 80.01 mIoU on the official test server, 0.5 points above DGFusion (79.5), and on MCubeS 59.78, 2.2 points above the best published result. We also analyse how the dependence on each sensor changes across conditions, where the high-resolution path helps, and where the model still fails.
