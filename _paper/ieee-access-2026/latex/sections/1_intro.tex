\PARstart{A}{utonomous} vehicles and mobile robots carry several sensors because each one fails in different conditions. A camera loses contrast at night and in fog, a depth estimate develops holes on reflective surfaces, an event camera goes silent on static scenes, and LiDAR returns thin out in rain and with distance. Multimodal semantic segmentation fuses these sensors so that the scene is still understood when one of them degrades.

The fusion models that lead the full-sensor benchmarks~\cite{cmnext2023,cmx2023,stitchfusion2024,geminifusion2024,cafuser2025,dgfusion2026} handle adverse conditions by telling the fusion what the condition is. CAFuser~\cite{cafuser2025} infers a condition token from the camera image and supervises it with a textual description of the weather and lighting, and DGFusion~\cite{dgfusion2026} adds local depth tokens and depth supervision. These models see the adverse scenes contained in their training data, but they are not trained with degraded or missing sensors, and they lose much of their accuracy when a sensor actually fails: on DELIVER, the released DGFusion weights fall to 7.5 mean intersection over union (mIoU) when only the RGB camera is left (Fig.~\ref{fig:robust}b). Methods designed for missing sensors~\cite{magic2024,anyseg2024,rmmss2025,fuseadapt2026} train with modality dropout and distillation, but the two lines of work rarely evaluate the same models under the same protocol~\cite{mmssbench2025}. Frozen vision foundation models offer strong features for every sensor, yet their multimodal adaptations mostly keep RGB as the primary stream and inject one auxiliary sensor~\cite{mmsamadapter2025}.

We ask whether a change of the training signal, instead of additional condition supervision, can give an accurate fusion model the ability to hold up when its sensors degrade. We propose Clean-to-Degraded Two-Pass Distillation (C2D2). Every training batch is used twice, once with clean inputs (the original samples of the dataset, adverse weather and night included, without synthetic corruption) and once with sensor-specific synthetic degradation, such as blur and exposure changes for the camera, holes for depth, dropped beams for LiDAR, and lost resolution for events, together with entire sensor removal. The degraded pass is supervised by the labels and distilled from a frozen teacher that sees the clean input (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). C2D2 needs no condition or degradation labels and adds no computation at inference. We apply it to a fusion model that reads every sensor with one frozen DINOv3 backbone~\cite{dinov3_2025} through sensor-specific low-rank adapters, fuses the sensors with cross-modal attention, and recovers thin structures with a high-resolution decoding path.

On DELIVER~\cite{cmnext2023}, the model reaches 57.74 mIoU on the test set (mean of three training runs), 1.0 point above DGFusion (56.7), the best published four-sensor fusion model, and under the public missing-sensor benchmark~\cite{mmssbench2025} its mIoU averaged over all subsets of removed sensors is 58.52 on the validation set, 13.6 points above the best method reported there. On the real MUSES benchmark, it reaches 80.01 mIoU on the official test server, 0.5 points above DGFusion (79.5), and on MCubeS 59.78, 2.2 points above the best published result. This accuracy comes from the model, that is, the frozen foundation backbone, cross-modal attention, and the high-resolution path; C2D2 keeps it (56.40 without and 56.59 with C2D2 on the DELIVER test set, before the high-resolution path is added) while raising the mIoU averaged over missing-sensor subsets by 9.8 points on the validation set (Table~\ref{tab:abl_robust}), and removing the degraded pass removes this gain (Table~\ref{tab:ablation_loo}). We also analyse how the dependence on each sensor changes across conditions, where the high-resolution path helps, and where the model still fails.

\begin{itemize}
\item We propose C2D2, a two-pass training scheme that uses every batch once with clean inputs and once with sensor-specific degradation or removed sensors, and distills the degraded pass from a frozen clean-input teacher at the output logits. Unlike condition-aware fusion, it needs no condition or degradation labels, and it adds no inference cost. Removing the degraded pass leaves accuracy with all sensors within the spread between training runs but lowers the average over missing-sensor subsets by 9.4 points, so the robustness comes from this pass.
\item We build the C2D2 model: one frozen DINOv3 backbone shared by all sensors with sensor-specific LoRA, cross-modal attention, and a high-resolution decoding path for thin structures. The architecture is accurate with all sensors, the most accurate four-sensor model on the DELIVER test set (57.74 mIoU, 1.0 point above DGFusion), and with the C2D2 training scheme, which keeps this accuracy, the same model is the most robust under the public missing-sensor benchmark~\cite{mmssbench2025} (58.52 mIoU averaged over the sensor subsets on the validation set, 13.6 points above MAGIC); on MUSES it reaches 80.01 mIoU on the official test server.
