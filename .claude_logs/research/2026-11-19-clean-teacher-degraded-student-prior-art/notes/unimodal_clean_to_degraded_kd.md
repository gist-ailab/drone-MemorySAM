# Single-modality prior work: clean-input teacher -> degraded-input student distillation

Scope: single-modality vision only. Verification legend: **[V]** = checked against the paper's full text / abstract in this session (alphaXiv PDF pages or arXiv abs page); **[M]** = from memory, not re-verified this session (treat as "unverified"). Venue names not printed in the retrieved arXiv pages are marked "(venue from memory)".

## Q1. Earliest and best-known papers that distill from clean-input teachers to students fed degraded inputs

### Takeaway
The exact structure (fixed teacher trained on clean data sees the clean image; student sees a synthetically degraded copy of the same image; loss = supervised loss + lambda * distillation loss on teacher output) was published as a named method, "Cross Quality Distillation (CQD)", by Su & Maji in April 2016 (arXiv 1604.00433), and has been re-used continuously since (low-resolution face recognition 2018-2024, adversarially robust distillation 2019-, foggy-scene segmentation 2017, adverse-weather depth 2023, corruption-robust detection / re-ID / ViT representation 2026). It is at least 10 years old and common.

### Cited Findings

**(A) Direct structural matches (fixed clean teacher -> degraded student, same sample)**

- **Su & Maji, "Adapting Models to Signal Degradation using Distillation"** (arXiv 1604.00433, v1 Apr 2016, v2 Aug 2017; BMVC 2017 - venue from memory, the PDF carries a "(c) 2017" BMVC-style copyright line). [V]
  - Teacher f: trained on high-quality data A, sees clean x. Student g: sees z = T(x), a synthetically degraded copy of the same sample ("It is easy to generate aligned data by applying known transformations T to obtain paired data of the form [x, T(x)]").
  - Objective (Eq. 1): sum L1(g(z_i), y_i) + lambda * sum L2(g(z_i), f(x_i)) + R(g), i.e. supervised loss + distillation loss, exactly the structure under review. L2 = cross-entropy on temperature-smoothed predictions (T=10; lambda=200 CUB / 50 CARS); squared error on logits "worked similarly".
  - Teacher: trained first, then fixed ("first train a model f ... and train a second model g ... by forcing an agreement"). Architecture: same (vgg-m -> vgg-m, student may be initialised from teacher) and different (vgg-vd -> vgg-m) both tested.
  - Degradations: low resolution (50x50 upsampled to 224), non-localized (uncropped) object, colour -> edge image, thin-plate-spline distortion, and combinations ("the type of degradation in domain B can be arbitrary, as long as we have the instance-level correspondence").
  - Task: fine-grained classification (CUB-200-2011, Stanford Cars). CQD beats training on degraded data, A+B data augmentation, and staged training on every setting (e.g. CUB low-res 61.0 -> 64.4; CARS low-res 41.6 -> 48.8).
  - The paper explicitly names the method "Cross Quality Distillation" and frames it as "a generalization of model compression where the inputs of the two functions are different". — [arXiv 1604.00433](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00433)
- **Peng, Hoffman, Yu, Saenko, "Fine-to-coarse knowledge transfer for low-res image classification"** (ICIP 2016): cited by Su & Maji as the prior "staged training" baseline (train on high-quality A, then fine-tune on low-quality B) - an even earlier high-res -> low-res transfer, but by weight initialisation rather than a distillation loss. Details beyond that citation are unverified. — [arXiv 1604.00433](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00433)
- **Sakaridis, Dai, Van Gool, "Semantic Foggy Scene Understanding with Synthetic Data"** (arXiv 1708.07819, v1 Aug 2017; IJCV 2018 - venue from memory). [V] Dense prediction. See Q4 for details: clear-weather segmentation model predicts on clear image x_j, its output y_hat_j = phi(x_j) supervises the model phi' that receives the synthetic-fog counterpart x'_j, in addition to the supervised loss on labeled foggy images (Eq. 9). The authors state "The supervision transfer from clear weather to foggy weather in this paper is inspired by the stream of work on model distillation/imitation". — [arXiv 1708.07819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07819)
- **Ge, Zhao, Li, Li, "Low-resolution Face Recognition in the Wild via Selective Knowledge Distillation"** (arXiv 1811.09998; DOI 10.1109/TIP.2018.2883743, IEEE TIP). [V abstract] Teacher: complex CNN on high-resolution faces. Student: much simpler CNN on resolution-degraded faces. Distilled: "most informative facial features" selected by sparse graph optimisation, used for feature regression + low-res face classification. Different architectures. — [arXiv 1811.09998](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.09998)
- **Goldblum, Fowl, Feizi, Goldstein, "Adversarially Robust Distillation (ARD)"** (arXiv 1905.09747; AAAI 2020 - venue from memory). [V]
  - Loss (Eq. 1): alpha * t^2 * KL(S(X + delta), T(X)) + (1 - alpha) * CE(S(X), y). Fig. 1 caption: "minimizing discrepancies between the outputs of a teacher on natural images and the outputs of a student on adversarial images."
  - "our method treats the teacher network's softmax output on clean data as the ground truth and trains a student network to reproduce this ground truth when exposed to adversarial examples."
  - Teacher fixed (logits can be pre-stored: "Our method does not require forward passes through the teacher during training as long as logits of the training data are stored ahead of time"). Main results use an adversarially trained teacher; Appendix C also runs ARD with a *naturally (clean) trained* teacher and still obtains robust students (ResNet18 -> MobileNetV2: 84.18% natural / 44.61% robust on CIFAR-10). Same-architecture self-distillation also tested (ResNet18 -> ResNet18).
  - They tried feeding the adversarial example to the teacher (T(x') instead of T(x)) and report that both natural and robust accuracy decreased.
  - Degradation = PGD l_inf adversarial perturbation; task = CIFAR-10/100 classification. — [arXiv 1905.09747](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09747)
- **Zi, Zhao, Ma, Jiang, "Revisiting Adversarial Robustness Distillation: Robust Soft Labels Make Student Better" (RSLAD)** (arXiv 2108.07969; ICCV 2021 - venue from memory). [V abstract] Robust (adversarially trained) teacher's soft labels guide the student on both natural and adversarial examples in all loss terms. Exact loss form not verified this session. — [arXiv 2108.07969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07969)
- **Ravula, Smyrnis, Jordan, Dimakis, "Inverse Problems Leveraging Pre-trained Contrastive Representations"** (arXiv 2110.07439; NeurIPS 2021). [V abstract] Pretrained representation network R(x) (e.g. CLIP) on clean images is the target; a student receives the corrupted image A(x) and is trained with a contrastive objective to recover R(x). Distortions: blur, additive noise, random pixel masking. Evaluated by linear probe on an ImageNet subset. — [arXiv 2110.07439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.07439)
- **Shin, Lee, Lee, Yu, Lee, "Teaching Where to Look: Attention Similarity Knowledge Distillation for Low Resolution Face Recognition" (A-SKD)** (arXiv 2209.14498; ECCV 2022). [V abstract] HR network (teacher) sees high-resolution faces, LR network (student) sees low-resolution faces; teacher attention maps are distilled with a cosine-similarity loss. (Authors are from GIST, Kyoobin Lee's group.) — [arXiv 2209.14498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14498)
- **Gasperini, Morbitzer, Jung, Navab, Tombari, "Robust Monocular Depth Estimation under Challenging Conditions" (md4all)** (arXiv 2308.09711; ICCV 2023 - venue from memory). [V] Dense prediction, see Q4. "md4all-DD: Day Distillation": frozen depth network B trained only on day-clear images receives the clean image e_i; the student DD receives m_i = a mix of clean and GAN-translated night/rain versions of the same image; loss L_d = mean |DD(m_i) - B(e_i)| / DD(m_i). — [arXiv 2308.09711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09711)
- **"Low-Resolution Face Recognition via Adaptable Instance-Relation Distillation"** (arXiv 2409.02049; CAS). [search-result abstract only] States that "Recent approaches based on knowledge distillation have proven that high-resolution clues can well guide low-resolution face recognition" - i.e. describes HR-teacher -> LR-student KD as an established line by 2024. Method details unverified. — [alphaXiv 2409.02049](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2409.02049)
- **Lee, Shin, Lee, Lee, "CD-FKD: Cross-Domain Feature Knowledge Distillation for Robust Single-Domain Generalization in Object Detection"** (arXiv 2603.16439, Mar 2026; LG Electronics / Naver / GIST; venue not stated in the PDF). [V] "The teacher network receives original source domain data, whereas the student network learns from diversified source data with various scales and corruptions." Teacher: pretrained on source, **frozen** during distillation; "self-distillation structure with two identical detectors" (Faster R-CNN R101-FPN). Distilled: global backbone features + instance-wise RoI features (cosine similarity), plus the detection loss. Degradations: 15 ImageNet-C-style corruptions (Gaussian/impulse/shot/speckle noise, defocus/glass/zoom/motion blur, contrast, brightness, elastic, JPEG, pixelate, spatter, saturate) at severity 1-5 + downscaling ratio 0.6-1.0. Task: single-domain generalised detection on the Diverse Weather benchmark (38.3 mAP50 average over 4 target domains vs 27.2 Faster R-CNN, 35.5 DivAlign). — [arXiv 2603.16439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16439)
- **Alexis, Giannopoulos, Gunopulos, "Distilling Vision Transformers for Distortion-Robust Representation Learning"** (arXiv 2604.22529, Apr 2026). [V] "we propose an asymmetric knowledge distillation framework in which both teacher and student are initialized from the same pretrained Vision Transformer but receive different views of each image: the teacher processes clean images, while the student sees their distorted versions." Teacher frozen (DINO ViT-B/16); same architecture. Distilled: CLS embedding (MSE) + patch tokens (MSE) + CLS attention maps (KL, tau=2). No labels during distillation (supervised fine-tuning afterwards). Distortions: 75/90% pixel masking, Gaussian noise sigma 0.3/0.5, Gaussian blur. Task: classification / transfer. The authors themselves say the use of KD "for learning effective representations under severe visual distortions remains underexplored", naming Ravula et al. 2021 as the notable exception - they do not cite Su & Maji 2016 in the retrieved pages. — [arXiv 2604.22529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22529)
- **Xie & Xu, "DARA: Degradation-Aware Low-Rank Residual Adaptation with Original-to-Corrupted Distillation for Corruption-Robust Animal Re-Identification"** (arXiv 2607.16644, Jul 2026). [V] "A frozen copy serves as the teacher ... the teacher embeds the original image, and the student embeds the corrupted view for distillation"; student also trained with standard Re-ID losses on original and corrupted views. Distilled: individual embedding (cosine) + hard pairwise relations. Same architecture (MobileNetV2), same checkpoint. Corruptions: 8 ImageNet-C operators (Gaussian noise, motion/defocus blur, fog, brightness, contrast, JPEG, pixelate). Its related work names two more person re-ID works with the same idea: Zhang et al. [35] "align corrupted person features with teacher representations from original inputs" and Zhao et al. [36] (titles/ids of [35], [36] not retrieved - unverified). — [arXiv 2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16644)

**(B) Near matches (same asymmetry, but teacher not a frozen clean-trained supervised model, or pseudo-labels on unlabeled data)**

- **Xie, Luong, Hovy, Le, "Self-training with Noisy Student improves ImageNet classification"** (arXiv 1911.04252; CVPR 2020 - venue from memory). [V] "the teacher produces high-quality pseudo labels by reading in clean images, while the student is required to reproduce those labels with augmented images as input." Teacher is un-noised and fixed within each iteration; student is noised with RandAugment, dropout, stochastic depth; soft or hard pseudo labels; student equal or larger than the teacher. Ablation: "adding noise to the teacher model that generates pseudo labels leads to lower accuracy". ImageNet-C mCE 45.7 -> 28.3. Difference from the structure under review: the distillation term is on 300M *unlabeled* images, the student is larger, and the process is iterated. — [arXiv 1911.04252](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04252)
- **Hoyer, Dai, Wang, Van Gool, "MIC: Masked Image Consistency for Context-Enhanced Domain Adaptation"** (arXiv 2212.01322; CVPR 2023 - venue from memory). [V] EMA teacher sees the complete target image, student sees the same image with random patches masked (patch 64, ratio 0.7) and must match the teacher's pseudo-label; "the teacher has privileged access to the original image". Teacher is EMA (not frozen), target domain is unlabeled. See Q4. — [arXiv 2212.01322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01322)
- **Yang et al., "Depth Anything"** (arXiv 2401.10891; CVPR 2024). [V abstract only] Abstract confirms a data engine that auto-annotates ~62M unlabeled images and "a more challenging optimization target ... by leveraging data augmentation tools". That the pseudo-labels come from a teacher on the un-perturbed image while the student gets strong colour/CutMix perturbations is from memory - unverified. — [arXiv 2401.10891](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.10891)
- **Amirkhani et al., "Robust Semantic Segmentation with Multi-Teacher Knowledge Distillation"** (IEEE Access 2021, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3107841). [search snippet only] Multi-teacher KD for a lightweight segmentation student evaluated on clean and corrupted data; which inputs teachers vs student receive is unverified. — [bohrium record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/robust-semantic-segmentation-with-multi-teacher-knowledge-distillation/811876383637635072-2649)
- **Massoli, Amato, Falchi, "Cross-Resolution Learning for Face Recognition"** (arXiv 1912.02851; Image and Vision Computing 99, 2020). [V abstract only] Abstract only says a SOTA face model is fine-tuned to "extract resolution-robust deep features"; that the training uses a teacher (HR) - student (LR) scheme is from memory - unverified. — [arXiv 1912.02851](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02851)

### Inferences
- The scheme under review (frozen clean-trained teacher on clean x; student on blur/exposure/noise/occlusion/dropped-region copy of x; KL or feature loss + ordinary supervised loss) is, loss-for-loss, Su & Maji's CQD Eq. 1 (2016) with a different degradation set, and equals ARD Eq. 1 (2019) with the adversarial perturbation replaced by a synthetic corruption. A novelty claim on the *structure itself* is not defensible in single-modality vision.
- The professor's "I have seen this before" is consistent with several candidates; the two closest in a robotics/vision lab context are A-SKD (ECCV 2022) and CD-FKD (2026), both from the GIST group of Kyoobin Lee, and CD-FKD uses frozen clean teacher + ImageNet-C-corrupted student + feature KD + task loss.
- Recent papers (2604.22529, 2607.16644, 2603.16439) still present this structure as a contribution in 2026, each for a new task/backbone, and none cites Su & Maji in the pages retrieved. So the idea is repeatedly re-proposed per task; novelty in such papers rests on the task, the distilled quantity (attention, relations, instance features), or added modules, not on the clean->degraded asymmetry.
- Consistent empirical lesson across ARD and Noisy Student: keeping the teacher on the clean input is better than giving the teacher the degraded input.

### Gaps
- QualNet (Kim et al., CVPR 2021, low-resolution/quality-agnostic face recognition), Zhu et al. "Low-resolution visual recognition via deep feature distillation" (ICASSP 2019), "bridge distillation" (Ge et al.), and DDL (Huang et al., ECCV 2020) were not retrieved; I recall them as belonging to the same HR->LR line but could not verify ids or mechanisms this session.
- No verified paper was found that applies a *frozen clean supervised teacher -> ImageNet-C-style corrupted student* scheme to ImageNet classification and reports ImageNet-C; closest verified items are Noisy Student (unlabeled data) and 2604.22529 (ImageNet-100, masking/noise/blur).
- Publication venues marked "(venue from memory)" were not printed in the retrieved arXiv pages.
- Person re-ID references [35] Zhang et al. and [36] Zhao et al. from the DARA paper could not be resolved to titles/ids.

## Q2. Works that do the same thing WITHOUT a separate teacher (clean/augmented consistency, self-distillation, mean teacher, DINO-style)

### Takeaway
A large family enforces clean-vs-degraded output consistency inside one network (or between a network and its own EMA): stability training, VAT/TRADES, AugMix JSD, UDA/FixMatch, Mean Teacher, DINO. They share the "same sample, two views, match outputs" idea but the target is produced by the model being trained (moving, not a converged clean expert), so the clean branch is not a fixed reference and usually also receives gradient or is a weight average of the student.

### Cited Findings
- **AugMix** (Hendrycks, Mu, Cubuk, Zoph, Gilmer, Lakshminarayanan; arXiv 1912.02781; ICLR 2020). [V] Loss: L(p_orig, y) + lambda * JS(p_orig; p_augmix1; p_augmix2), where all three posteriors come from the *same* network on the clean image and two AugMix-ed versions; the supervised CE is applied on the clean view only. No separate teacher, symmetric JSD (gradient flows through the clean branch). CIFAR-10-C error 26.9 (standard) -> 11.2 (WRN); ablation: JSD takes Random-AutoAugment from 17.0 -> 14.7 and AugmentAndMix from 13.1 -> 11.2. ImageNet-C mCE 80.6 -> 68.4 (ResNet-50). — [arXiv 1912.02781](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02781)
- **Stability training** (Zheng, Song, Leung, Goodfellow, "Improving the robustness of deep neural networks via stability training", CVPR 2016): cited by AugMix as a precedent for the consistency loss ("The Jensen-Shannon Consistency Loss impels the model to be stable, consistent, and insensitive across a diverse range of inputs (Bachman et al., 2014; Zheng et al., 2016; Kannan et al., 2018)"). That it pairs a clean image with a Gaussian-noise-perturbed copy in a single network is from memory - unverified; arXiv id not retrieved. — [arXiv 1912.02781](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02781)
- **VAT / TRADES**: as described in the ARD paper, they "use as a loss function a linear combination of cross-entropy loss and KL divergence between the network's softmax output from clean input and from adversarial input ... The KL divergence term acts as a consistency regularizer" - same network on both inputs, no teacher. ARD positions itself as the teacher-based analogue. — [arXiv 1905.09747](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09747)
- **Consistency-training SSL vs a fixed teacher**: Noisy Student states the key difference explicitly: "consistency regularization works less well on ImageNet because consistency regularization uses a model being trained to generate the pseudo-labels. In the early phase of training, they regularize the model towards high entropy predictions"; pseudo-label methods "rely on a model being trained instead of a converged model with high accuracy to generate pseudo labels". It also cites UDA [91] as the closest consistency method ("similar to UDA"). — [arXiv 1911.04252](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04252)
- **Mean Teacher / EMA teacher**: MIC describes its teacher as "implemented as an EMA teacher [82] ... a temporal ensemble of previous student models", updated from the student each step (phi <- alpha*phi + (1-alpha)*theta), "a common strategy used in semi-supervised learning and UDA". MIC ablation: replacing EMA predictions with the model's own predictions costs -0.7 mIoU. — [arXiv 2212.01322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01322)
- **DINO / DINOv2**: described in 2604.22529 as "self-distillation with multi-crop augmentation"; that paper contrasts its own frozen-teacher, clean-vs-distorted "asymmetric" setup with these SSL methods. — [arXiv 2604.22529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22529)
- Su & Maji place CQD relative to plain data augmentation (train on A+B) and show the distillation term adds on top of it (CUB low-res: A+B 62.2 vs CQD 64.4; CARS: 47.3 vs 48.8). — [arXiv 1604.00433](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00433)

### Inferences
- Three distinguishable regimes: (i) single-network symmetric consistency (AugMix JSD, stability training, TRADES/VAT, UDA) - no teacher, clean branch is the same weights; (ii) EMA/self-distillation (Mean Teacher, FixMatch-style weak->strong, DINO, MIC) - teacher is a moving average of the student, improves with it, usually used on unlabeled data; (iii) frozen converged clean expert (CQD, ARD, md4all-DD, CD-FKD, 2604.22529, DARA) - the target is a fixed function of the clean image, can be precomputed, and cannot drift toward the degraded-input solution. The scheme under review is regime (iii).
- The differences from regime (i)/(ii) that can honestly be stated: fixed target (no collapse / no co-adaptation), teacher quality independent of student, logits can be cached; but these are properties of the known regime (iii), not new.

### Gaps
- FixMatch (arXiv 2001.07685), UDA (1904.12848), Mean Teacher (1703.01780), DINO (2104.14294) were not opened this session; ids and the weak/strong description are from memory - unverified.
- No head-to-head study was retrieved comparing frozen-clean-teacher KD vs AugMix-JSD vs EMA teacher under the same corruption set for supervised single-modality training.

## Q3. Is there any standard name for this structure in the literature?

### Takeaway
There is no single standard name; each sub-community coined its own. The oldest explicit name is "Cross Quality Distillation (CQD)" (2016); others are "supervision transfer" (2017), "cross-resolution / HR-to-LR distillation" (face recognition), "adversarially robust distillation" (2019), "day distillation" (2023), "cross-domain (feature) knowledge distillation" (2026), "asymmetric knowledge distillation" (2026), "original-to-corrupted distillation" (2026). Theoretically it is an instance of learning using privileged information / generalized distillation.

### Cited Findings
- "We call our approach Cross Quality Distillation (CQD)"; framed as "(i) a generalization of model compression where the inputs of the two functions are different, (ii) a specialization of domain adaptation when z can be synthesized from x". — [arXiv 1604.00433](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00433)
- Relation to LUPI: Su & Maji discuss "Learning with privileged information (LUPI)" and note "Lopez-Paz et al. [35] made a similar observation unifying distillation and learning with privileged information" (Lopez-Paz, Bottou, Schoelkopf, Vapnik, "Unifying distillation and privileged information", ICLR 2016). MIC also uses the wording "the teacher has privileged access to the original image". — [arXiv 1604.00433](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00433); [arXiv 2212.01322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01322)
- "unsupervised supervision transfer from clear-weather images to their synthetic foggy counterparts". — [arXiv 1708.07819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07819)
- "Adversarially Robust Distillation (ARD)". — [arXiv 1905.09747](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09747)
- "md4all-DD: Day Distillation". — [arXiv 2308.09711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09711)
- "Cross-Domain Feature Knowledge Distillation (CD-FKD)". — [arXiv 2603.16439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16439)
- "asymmetric knowledge distillation framework". — [arXiv 2604.22529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22529)
- "original-to-corrupted distillation". — [arXiv 2607.16644](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16644)
- "Cross-resolution knowledge distillation" is used as a term in 2026 pathology work (high-magnification -> low-magnification). — [alphaXiv 2609.36407](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.36407)

### Inferences
- When citing, the safest umbrella wording is "cross-quality (clean-to-degraded) distillation, a form of distillation with privileged (clean) teacher input", with Su & Maji 2016 as the origin and ARD 2019 as the best-known instance.
- Fragmented naming explains why the idea is repeatedly re-proposed and why a keyword search under one name misses the others.

### Gaps
- No survey that unifies these lines under one term was found (the corruption-robustness survey arXiv 2305.06024 surfaced in search but was not opened).

## Q4. Which of these are for dense prediction (semantic segmentation) specifically?

### Takeaway
Dense-prediction instances exist since 2017: Sakaridis et al. (clear-weather segmentation predictions supervising the synthetic-fog copy), MIC 2023 (EMA teacher on full image, student on masked image; segmentation + detection), md4all 2023 (frozen day-clear depth teacher, adverse-translated student). A frozen clean supervised teacher + synthetic photometric/occlusion corruption + KL on logits specifically for *supervised semantic segmentation* was not found verified in single-modality work this session; the closest are Sakaridis 2017 (fog only, pseudo-label CE) and CD-FKD 2026 (detection, features).

### Cited Findings
- **Sakaridis et al. 2017/2018 (semantic segmentation + detection, Foggy Cityscapes)**. [V] Semi-supervised scheme: a clear-weather model phi is used to label clear images x_j; the foggy model phi' is trained with min sum_i L(phi'(x'_i), y_i) + lambda * sum_j L(phi'(x'_j), y_hat_j), y_hat_j = phi(x_j) (Eq. 9), L = categorical cross-entropy, lambda = (l/u)*w with w=5. Motivation quoted: "1) in clear weather, objects are easier to recognize than in foggy weather ...; 2) since the synthetic foggy images and their clear-weather counterparts depict exactly the same scene, recognition results should also be the same for both images ideally." Teacher fixed; same architecture (RefineNet). Degradation: physically-based synthetic fog (beta=0.01). Result on Foggy Driving: 44.3 (clear model) -> 46.3 (supervised fine-tune on 498 foggy) -> 49.7 mIoU (with supervision transfer on 20,000 pairs). Note the transferred pairs are *unlabeled*; labeled images only get the GT loss. — [arXiv 1708.07819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07819)
- **MIC (semantic segmentation, detection, classification; UDA)**. [V] L_M = q_T * H(f_theta(M * x_T), p_T), p_T = argmax of EMA teacher on the unmasked target image. Benchmarks include clear-to-adverse-weather (Cityscapes -> ACDC +2.4 mIoU, -> DarkZurich +4.3, -> FoggyZurich +3.7 over HRDA). Ablation: without masking (only colour augmentation on the student) the gain disappears (70.6 -> 50.6 in their Table 7 row 3); for supervised training MIC gives only +0.3 mIoU vs +2.3 for UDA. For night targets they *disable* colour augmentation because it "can corrupt the content of dark nighttime images". — [arXiv 2212.01322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01322)
- **md4all (monocular depth, nuScenes / RobotCar)**. [V] Frozen day-clear baseline B gives depth on the clean image, student gets x% GAN-translated night/rain copies; inference unchanged, single model. Ablation (nuScenes val, absRel day / night / rain): baseline 0.1333 / 0.2419 / 0.1572 -> md4all-DD 0.1366 / 0.1921 / 0.1414; distillation variant (DD) beats the variant that only computes losses on the clean image (AD) on nuScenes. Training on clean and deploying on adverse is reported to be more effective than naively training on both, for the self-supervised losses. — [arXiv 2308.09711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09711)
- **CD-FKD (object detection, dense features)**. [V] See Q1; ablation: corruption+downscale augmentation alone 27.2 -> 30.9 average mAP50, adding global + instance feature KD from the frozen clean teacher -> 38.3; source-domain mAP50 also rises 54.9 -> 62.7. — [arXiv 2603.16439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16439)
- **Amirkhani et al. 2021 (robust semantic segmentation, multi-teacher KD)**: exists (IEEE Access, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2021.3107841) and evaluates on clean and corrupted data; input assignment unverified. — [bohrium record](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/robust-semantic-segmentation-with-multi-teacher-knowledge-distillation/811876383637635072-2649)
- **RFKD (Chen et al., arXiv 2404.06258)**: search snippet says it "mixes clean and noisy inputs during distillation to transfer robust features" for crack segmentation; details unverified. — [arXiv 2404.06258](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06258v1.pdf)
- Recent adverse-weather segmentation works found by search (2026: WeatherSeg 2604.22824 "teacher-student dual learning", LiDSeg 2611.20864, UG2+ challenge reports 2605.27962 / 2605.22216, generalizable KD from VFMs 2603.02554) were listed but not opened - their mechanisms are unverified. — [alphaXiv 2604.22824](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.22824); [alphaXiv 2603.02554](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.02554)

### Inferences
- For dense prediction the structure is established for fog (2017), nighttime/rain depth (2023), masking in UDA (2023) and corruption-robust detection (2026). A remaining narrow niche, as far as this search verified, is: supervised (labeled) semantic segmentation with a frozen clean-trained teacher and per-pixel KL on logits under a mixed synthetic corruption set. That is an application gap, not a structural novelty; a reviewer can map it directly onto CQD/ARD/Sakaridis.
- MIC's ablation (masking is what matters; photometric augmentation alone on the student does not help in UDA) and md4all's AD-vs-DD comparison are the most relevant prior evidence on *which degradations* and *whether a distilled target beats GT-only supervision on the degraded copy*.

### Gaps
- GCMA / MGCDA / Refign (day-night correspondence-guided segmentation by Sakaridis et al. and Bruggemann et al.) are related clean-reference -> adverse-image guidance methods but use *real* cross-time image pairs rather than synthetic degradation; not retrieved this session - unverified.
- Could not verify any single-modality semantic-segmentation paper that reports Cityscapes-C / ACDC with exactly "frozen clean teacher on clean image, student on synthetically corrupted copy, KL on logits + CE". Absence in ~25 tool calls is not proof of absence.

## How old and how common is this structure (explicit statement)

- **Age**: at least 10.5 years as of Nov 2026. First explicit, named formulation with the identical loss form: Su & Maji, arXiv April 2016 (BMVC 2017). A staged high-res -> low-res transfer precursor (Peng et al., ICIP 2016) and the LUPI/generalized-distillation theory (Lopez-Paz et al., ICLR 2016) are contemporaneous. First dense-prediction instance: Sakaridis et al., Aug 2017. — [arXiv 1604.00433](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.00433); [arXiv 1708.07819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.07819)
- **Commonness**: verified instances this session span classification (2016, 2019, 2021, 2026), face recognition (2018, 2022, 2024), segmentation (2017, 2023), depth (2023), detection (2026), re-ID (2026) - 11 direct instances (7 verified from full text, 4 from abstracts only) and 2 near matches (Noisy Student, MIC) with the clean-teacher / degraded-student asymmetry, plus the adversarial-robustness-distillation sub-field built on ARD. It is a standard, repeatedly rediscovered technique, not a rare one.
- **What is still claimed as new in 2026 papers**: the task, the distilled signal (attention maps, pairwise relations, instance RoI features), and add-on modules (routed LoRA experts) - not the asymmetry itself.
